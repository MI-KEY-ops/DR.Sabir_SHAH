# **My Transformative Journey Through the Certificate in Health Professions Education (CHPE) Course** 

## **1. Introduction** 

I have greatly benefited from and been enriched by my experiences in the Certificate in Health Professions Education (CHPE) course. Prior to enrolling in this course, my main area of work was clinical duties, handling patients and diagnosing, treating diseases at Bajaur Clinic. I thought it was enough to be a competent healthcare professional, have enough medical knowledge and clinical skills. But the course slowly sparked my interest in another aspect of healthcare education, and made me realize that great healthcare workers are great teachers, communicators, mentors, assessors and lifelong learners. 

I've had a great deal of personal growth and professional development during this course. I acquired skills on the relationship between educational theories and clinical experiences, selfevaluation of decision making, understanding feedback from others and giving it in a constructive way, and understanding the importance of evidence-based teaching and learning. Most importantly, I came to the realization that education doesn't just have to be the transfer of information, it has to be an experience that allows for meaningful learning to contribute to my professional growth and enhance patient outcomes. 

This reflective account is a way to talk about my experience of change, challenges, learning and skill building, and what I plan to do with my learning in my future professional work. 

## **2. Changing My Understanding of Teaching and Learning** 

My initial approach to teaching in the CHPE course was quite traditional, focusing on conveying medical information for students to memorize. I believed that any health care expert could effectively teach. However, this perspective shifted as I explored health professions education concepts, realizing that effective teaching must be thoroughly planned, address specific learning needs, involve appropriate strategies, engage learners actively, and assess learning outcomes. Embracing a learner-centered approach transformed my understanding, emphasizing the importance of what learners grasp and how they apply their knowledge. This shift in perspective also impacted my interactions with diabetes patients at Bajaur Clinic. I learned that simply informing them about blood glucose control was insufficient; they needed practical guidance on medication, diet, exercise, blood sugar monitoring, and foot care. I adopted simpler language, encouraged questions, and checked for comprehension using communication strategies and teach-back methods. This journey has deepened my 

understanding that true learning occurs when students can apply their knowledge effectively in real-world situations. 

## **3. The Importance of Reflective Practice** 

Learning to practice reflection was a key benefit of the CHPE course, significantly enhancing my clinical approach. Previously, my focus was limited to patients' diagnoses, treatments, and outcomes, neglecting my own emotional responses and professional growth areas. Reflective writing assignments introduced me to Gibbs' Reflective Cycle, which involves description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion, and action planning. 

Applying this model to various cases at Bajaur clinic, including patients with Parkinson's disease, diabetes, prostate enlargement, pregnancy, and measles, I gained deeper insights. A notable experience was with a 3-year-old boy suspected of having measles; my initial worries emphasized the complexity of such cases, extending beyond clinical management to include infection control, parent education, proper referrals, and community protection. 

This scenario underscored the importance of effective communication with parents regarding vaccination and isolation. Reflective practice has taught me that mistakes or uncertainties are not failures but opportunities for growth when approached constructively. I've come to understand that reflection should be integral to my practice, transcending academic requirements and becoming a habitual part of my professional life. 

## **4. Developing Professionalism and Ethical Responsibility** 

One of the other key changes in my life throughout this class has been my knowledge of professionalism in healthcare. 

In the past, I had the impression of professionalism as punctuality, proper conduct, and compliance with medical guidelines. Thanks to CHPE though, I gained an appreciation of the wider aspects of this, such as accountability, confidentiality, respect for patients, informed consent, ethical decision making, team working and continuous professional development. 

In my experience, sometimes I had to manage patients in a challenging manner due to lack of resources for care. Through these experiences I learnt the importance of acknowledging my own limitations as a professional and to seek help and/or referral where required. 

A dilemma of the professionals was a young child suspected of having measles. The parents wished that the child should stay in the general waiting room, but I knew that the child could spread infection to others in the waiting room. I had to remember to communicate in a respectful way while keeping in mind my duty to keep infection control measures in place. 

Following the discussion with a senior colleague, I was given feedback that I need to improve my communication, have a better system for documenting clinical findings and follow infection-prevention protocols. 

This experience has taught me the importance of making responsible decisions even when they are difficult, which is what professionalism entails. 

## **5. Learning the Importance of Feedback** 

The CHPE course transformed my attitude towards feedback. I previously viewed it as criticism and failed to provide structured feedback to others. Now I understand constructive feedback is vital for professional development. I learned effective feedback must be timely, specific, respectful, and supportive. I also gained skills in receptiveness, active listening, and empathy for learner needs. Colleague feedback highlighted areas for improvement, including communication, documentation, confidence, and decision making. I hope to foster a culture where colleagues and learners comfortably discuss strengths and weaknesses. I now view feedback not as evidence of ability, but as a chance to grow. 

## **6. Understanding Assessment in Health Professions Education** 

Another valuable learning experience was that of learning the principles of assessment. Prior to this course, I had only thought of assessment in terms of examinations and written assessments. But I discovered that assessment is much more than that, and should include knowledge, clinical skills, attitude, communication and professional behaviour. 

Learned about formative and summative assessment. 

Formative assessment offers learning opportunities for ongoing improvement with opportunities for feedback and summative assessment determines whether learners have met the required learning outcomes. 

I also acknowledged the importance of being valid, reliable, fair, transparent, and feasible when it comes to assessment. 

At Bajaur Clinic, I came to understand that the evaluation of a healthcare learner is not solely based on theoretical learning. A learner who knows the management of the diabetes in an exam will often know how to deal with the diabetes but fail to communicate well with a patient or to give good counselling. 

## **7. Development of Research and Critical Thinking Skills** 

The CHPE course's research component was crucial to my transformation. Initially inexperienced with research questions, qualitative methods, and critical assessment, I developed a stronger grasp of methodology through the qualitative research workbook on diabetes. Clinical experiences at Bajaur clinic, where patients struggled with controlling their diabetes, medication, diet, and lifestyle, deepened my interest in the subject. I realized clinical management is not the only way to interpret lab results, as patient beliefs, family support, finances, and knowledge significantly affect disease management. Broadening my understanding of qualitative research allowed me to appreciate patient views and experiences. I also gained insight into literature reviews and evidence-based practice, learning to evaluate published research, compare perspectives, and use reliable evidence for clinical and educational decisions rather than accepting information on faith. 

## **8. Understanding Educational Evaluation Models** 

The course also took me to a new world of educational evaluation, and the need to establish whether or not educational programs deliver the desired effects. 

The use of Kirkpatrick's Four-Level Evaluation Model in the evaluation of a diabetes self management education program was especially helpful. 

I have been told there are four levels to educational programs evaluation: 

- 1.Reaction: Learners' response to the educational programme. 

- 2.Learning: What learners acquire in terms of knowledge, skills and attitudes. 

3. Behaviour: How learners behave based on learning. 

4. Results – whether the program is achieving outcomes that are meaningful. 

It has been useful for me to realise that there are other ways of judging the success of teaching than learner satisfaction or attendance. The change in behaviour and improvement in care also needs to be decided. 

For instance, diabetes education should not just be about knowledge, but also promoting adherence to medication, healthy lifestyles, frequent glucose testing and foot care practices. 

## **9. Challenges and Personal Growth** 

There were some difficulties during my time on the CHPE course. The demands of clinical duties, study preparation, research, and personal commitments proved to be a significant challenge in terms of time management and commitment. 

The terminology of education, research concepts, and theoretical frameworks were sometimes difficult to grasp, as my previous professional experiences involved dealing with clinical practice and not formal educations. 

At times, it was difficult to relate theory to practice. These challenges, however, were overcome in the process of working through clinical reflections and assignments. 

I slowly gained in confidence in academic writing, critical thinking, research and reflection. 

Another obstacle was the realization of my own weakness. I learned to be honest and to ask for advice about my own weaknesses and the need for professional development. 

I learned patience, discipline, adaptability and a life-long learning mindset from those challenges. 

## **10. Transformation in My Professional Identity** 

The biggest impact this course has had on me is my changing profession. 

Prior to CHPE, I was mainly a healthcare worker who was focused on patient care. I understand that I have a role to play in supporting learners, sharing knowledge, promoting evidence-based practice and in supporting healthcare education. 

I have grown in my understanding of the link between clinical competence and educational competence. 

I now feel that a healthcare professional ought to not only know how to treat a patient but also be able to explain clinical decisions, educate patients, support others and assist in the development of future healthcare professionals. 

This experience has strengthened my conviction of the need to fulfill this role, especially in places where health care may be less accessible and education less available. 

It has also further my desire to become a skilled medical specialist. Now I know that, specialization is not just about clinical knowledge but also about leadership, communication, teaching, research, and responsibility. 

## **11. My Future Action Plan** 

Based on this learning, I have a few learning goals for the future that I have developed. 

Next, I will persist in using reflective practice to assess and review my clinical decisions, draw learning conclusions and enhance patient care. 

Second, I will practice learner-centred teaching methods in patient education, students and colleagues education. I will actively engage students in participation, clinical discussions and practical learning. 

Third, I will actively look for and give constructive feedback to ensure a process of continuous professional development. 

Fourth, I will develop my research skills and regularly use up to date scientific information to bolster my clinical and educational decision making. 

Fifth, I will support patients, uphold professional standards, work collaboratively and communicate effectively in my work environment. 

Lastly, I will strive to support the work of health education and awareness in the Bajaur community, especially in the field of diabetes, vaccination against childhood diseases and prevention of common diseases. 

I also hope to pursue professional development and acquire the information and expertise necessary to be an efficient medical specialist and health educator. 

## **12. Conclusion** 

My CHPE course experience was transformative, shifting my perspective on healthcare education and professional responsibilities. I moved beyond simple teaching concepts to grasp reflective practice, learner-centered education, constructive feedback, evaluation, research, and assessment. Clinical work at Bajaur Clinic allowed me to apply theory to practice, revealing my strengths, weaknesses, and duties. Becoming a competent healthcare professional is a lifelong journey requiring reflection, humility, evidence-based practice, and continuous learning. Initially focused on treating patients, I now embrace the bigger picture: becoming a reflective practitioner, effective educator, supportive colleague, and responsible specialist contributing positively to patients, learners, and the healthcare community. My mindset has shifted from provider to one who teaches, learns, reflects, and improves healthcare practice throughout my career.

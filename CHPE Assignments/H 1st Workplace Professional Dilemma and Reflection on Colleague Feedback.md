# **Workplace Professional Dilemma and Reflection on Colleague Feedback** 

## **1. Description of the Professional Dilemma** 

In my clinical practice, I had a professional dilemma when I saw a young boy suspected of having measles in Bajaur Clinic. He was brought with his parents with fever, cough and generalized skin rash. Measles is a highly contagious infectious disease and I suspected it. Parents were faced with the dilemma of the child staying in the general waiting area as they had no knowledge of the risk of transmission. But I had a duty as a professional to ensure that other patients, healthcare workers and the community were kept safe from infection. I also wanted to keep the parents in a respectful relationship without embarrassing or discriminating against them. It was a hard decision for me to make between honouring the parents' request and providing proper infection control. I chose to use the opportunity to give a brief explanation of the contagious nature of measles, to separate the child from other patients, and to counsel the parents about appropriate precautions and further management. 

## **2. Colleague's Feedback** 

I spoke with another senior member of the clinic to get professional input. My colleague felt that I had prioritised patient and community safety. He, however, urged me to be more assertive and to explain the infection-control measures in layman's terms, in a way that parents could easily understand. He also stressed the need to adhere to routine infection control measures, record clinical observations, notify appropriate healthcare personnel and make appropriate referral if any danger signs were present. He said it would be beneficial to educate parents about routine immunization to help avoid future situations like this. 

## **3. Reflection on the Dilemma and Feedback** 

I felt uncomfortable about bothering the parents or breaking their trust, and that was at the start. Meanwhile, I knew that if I left a child who was potentially infectious in the midst of other patients, then there was a risk of establishing an infection in vulnerable patients. When I got my colleague's comments, I realised that sometimes in my professional life I will have to make tough choices that may not be readily accepted by patients or their families. I learnt that good health care practice involves more than just the diagnosis and treatment of disease. Also requires ethical decision making, effective communication, team working, confidentiality and patient safety. I learned through my colleague's feedback to be more aware of my communication skills and be confident in handling challenging situations. I also knew how crucial it was to get input from experts who have already done it, instead of trying to make hard choices on my own. 

## **4. Action Plan** 

For my future clinical practice: 

1. Do adhere to standard precautions for infection prevention and control. 

2. Enhance abilities in communicating with patients and/or families when explaining medical conditions. 

3. Respect the dignity, privacy and cultural norms of patients. 

4. Take advice from more senior professionals on complex professional issues. 

5. Keep good clinical records and refer promptly as needed. 

7. Encourage community education about childhood immunisation and infectious disease prevention. 

## **5. Conclusion** 

This experience was to make me realize that work dilemmas are an essential element of healthcare practice. By reviewing the feedback from my colleague, I was able to see how I could improve my communication skills, ethical decision making and how to improve patient safety. This experience will help me to become a more responsible, confident, and compassionate health care practitioner.
